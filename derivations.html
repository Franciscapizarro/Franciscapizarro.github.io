The poster states that the scheme is provably energy-stable. This is the argument behind that claim: an energy estimate for the projected system that fixes how large the SAT penalty matrix must be.

Setup

Take the projected system with homogeneous Dirichlet data at the outflow boundary,

$$ \frac{\partial \mathbf{a}}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(x\,\mathbf{V}\,\mathbf{a}\big) + \boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{a}), \qquad \mathbf{a}(t,X) = \mathbf{g}_1(t), $$

where \(\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^\top\) is the stochastic Galerkin advection matrix (the superscript \(L\) is dropped throughout). Spatial derivatives use an SBP operator \(\mathbf{D} = \mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{Q}\) with symmetric positive definite norm matrix \(\mathbf{P}\), satisfying

$$ \mathbf{Q} + \mathbf{Q}^\top = \mathbf{E}_N - \mathbf{E}_0 =: \mathbf{B}, $$

with \(\mathbf{E}_N = \mathrm{diag}(0,\dots,0,1)\) and \(\mathbf{E}_0 = \mathrm{diag}(1,0,\dots,0)\). The boundary condition is imposed weakly through \(\text{SAT} = -(\mathbf{P}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{I})(\mathbf{E}_N\otimes\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m)(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{g}_1)\).

The energy estimate

Define the discrete energy in the SBP norm,

$$ E(t) = \tfrac12\|\mathbf{a}\|_{\mathbf{P}}^2 = \tfrac12\,\mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{P}\otimes\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{a}. $$

Differentiating in time and substituting the semi-discrete PIDE — keeping only the transport and boundary-penalty terms, since the burst operator is treated separately — gives

$$ \frac{dE}{dt} = \mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{Q}\otimes\mathbf{I})(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{E}_N\otimes\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m)(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{g}_1), $$

with \(\mathcal{X} = \mathrm{diag}(x_1,\dots,x_N)\).

The transport term collapses to the boundaries

This is where SBP earns its keep. Because \(\mathbf{V}\) is symmetric, the quadratic form can be symmetrised and the SBP property applied:

$$ \begin{aligned} 2\,\mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{Q}\otimes\mathbf{I})(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} &= \mathbf{a}^\top\big((\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{Q}^\top)\otimes\mathbf{I}\big)(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} \\ &= \mathbf{a}^\top\big((\mathbf{E}_N-\mathbf{E}_0)\otimes\mathbf{I}\big)(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a}, \end{aligned} $$

so that

$$ \mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{Q}\otimes\mathbf{I})(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} = \tfrac12\,\mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{E}_N\otimes x_N\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} - \tfrac12\,\mathbf{a}^\top(\mathbf{E}_0\otimes x_1\mathbf{V})\mathbf{a} . $$

Why the left boundary vanishes. The advection speed is \(\gamma_x x\), which is zero at \(x_1 = 0\). The origin is a degenerate characteristic point: no information enters the domain there, so no boundary condition is needed and no energy is injected. Only the outflow boundary at \(x_N = L_X\) has to be controlled.

With homogeneous data \(\mathbf{g}_1 = \mathbf{0}\), collecting the right-boundary node,

$$ \frac{dE}{dt} = \mathbf{a}_N^\top\Big(\tfrac12 x_N\mathbf{V} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m\Big)\mathbf{a}_N , $$

where \(\mathbf{a}_N\) is the vector of stochastic coefficients at \(x = x_N\).

The penalty condition

Energy stability requires \(dE/dt \le 0\) for every \(\mathbf{a}_N\), which holds exactly when the bracket is negative semi-definite:

$$ \boxed{\;\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m \succeq \tfrac12\, x_N\, \mathbf{V}\;} $$

This is a matrix inequality, not a scalar one, and that is the point worth making. In the deterministic problem the penalty is a single number tied to the advection speed at the boundary. Under the Galerkin projection the advection matrix \(\mathbf{V}\) has entries \(\langle\gamma_x\Phi_k\Phi_j\rangle\), and the condition is on its whole spectrum: taking \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m = \tfrac12 x_N \lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{V})\mathbf{I}\) is the simplest admissible choice. The same spectrum sets the CFL restriction,

$$ \Delta t \le C\,\frac{\Delta x}{\max_i |\lambda_i(\mathbf{V})|} , $$

so widening the uncertainty in \(\gamma_x\) spreads the spectrum of \(\mathbf{V}\), which tightens the time step and raises the required penalty at the same time. Time integration is explicit fourth-order Runge–Kutta.

Scope. The estimate covers the transport and boundary treatment. The burst operator \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}\) is linear and mass-preserving but is not included in this bound; the analysis above is the transport-stability argument, not a full well-posedness proof of the coupled system.
