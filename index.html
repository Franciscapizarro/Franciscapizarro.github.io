This page holds the material that did not fit on the poster: the convergence and cost study behind the numerical scheme, and the control law in full.

The framework applies to gene regulatory networks of \(n\) interacting genes. A partial integro-differential equation (PIDE) governs the joint probability density of the protein levels, with kinetic parameters treated as random variables. An intrusive polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) and a Galerkin projection turn the stochastic PIDE into a coupled deterministic system for the PCE coefficients, discretised with summation-by-parts (SBP) operators and simultaneous-approximation-term (SAT) boundary conditions.

Two networks are used as test cases: a single self-regulating gene, and a two-gene toggle switch with mutual repression. The joint density is discretised on an \(N^n\) grid, which places the practical reach at small networks, on the order of three or four genes.

The model

A single gene expresses one protein type \(X\) through its mRNA. Because mRNA degrades much faster than protein (\(\gamma_m \gg \gamma_x\)), protein arrives in uncorrelated bursts of mean size \(b = k_x/\gamma_m\), exponentially distributed. Under that assumption the protein probability density \(p(t,x)\) obeys the PIDE of Friedman et al., extended to the multivariate case by Pájaro et al.:

$$ \frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(t,x) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big[\gamma_x\, x\, p(t,x)\big] + k_m \int_0^{x} \beta(x-y)\, c(y)\, p(t,y)\, \mathrm{d}y , $$

with burst kernel \(\beta(x-y) = b^{-1}e^{-(x-y)/b} - \delta(x-y)\). The first term is degradation; the integral is burst production. The feedback enters through \(c(x) = [1-\rho(x)] + \varepsilon\,\rho(x)\) with the Hill function \(\rho(x) = x^{H}/(x^{H}+K^{H})\): a negative \(H\) means \(X\) activates its own transcription, which is the cooperative positive-feedback case that produces bimodal distributions.

Extension to \(n\) interacting genes

The single-gene equation above is the \(n=1\) case of a multivariate PIDE. For a network of \(n\) genes, each expressing one protein, the state is the joint density \(p(t,\mathbf{x})\) over the whole protein vector \(\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\), and every species contributes one degradation term and one burst term:

$$ \frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(t,\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\big[\gamma_x^i\, x_i\, p(t,\mathbf{x})\big] + \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(k_m^i \int_0^{x_i} \beta_i(x_i-y_i)\, c_i(\mathbf{y}_i)\, p(t,\mathbf{y}_i)\dd y_i\right), $$

with \(p:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\}\) the temporal evolution of the protein probability density function, and each species carrying its own burst kernel

$$ \beta_i(x_i-y_i) = \frac{1}{b_i}\exp\!\left[-\frac{x_i-y_i}{b_i}\right] - \delta(x_i-y_i) . $$

Every kinetic parameter is now indexed by species: degradation \(\gamma_x^i\), transcription \(k_m^i\), mean burst size \(b_i = k_x^i/\gamma_m^i\). Each burst integral runs over one coordinate only, the \(i\)-th, with the others held fixed — \(\mathbf{y}_i\) denotes the state vector with its \(i\)-th component replaced by the integration variable \(y_i\),

$$ \mathbf{y}_i = (x_1,\dots,x_{i-1},\,y_i,\,x_{i+1},\dots,x_n) . $$

Where the coupling enters. The degradation terms are separable and each integral acts on a single coordinate, so the sums themselves do not couple the species. The coupling is contained in \(c_i\), which depends on the other proteins' levels. For a two-gene toggle switch, \(c_1\) is a decreasing Hill function of \(x_2\) and \(c_2\) of \(x_1\). If every \(c_i\) depends only on \(x_i\), the system reduces to \(n\) independent copies of the one-dimensional problem.

The joint density is discretised on an \(N^n\) grid, and the Galerkin projection below multiplies that system by the number of retained PCE coefficients. The results reported on this site are the one-dimensional case; the control section gives the two-dimensional toggle switch formulation.

Where the uncertainty goes

The kinetic parameters are not known exactly — measurement error and cell-to-cell variability both contribute. They are modelled as random variables. Two cases are studied:

Galerkin projection

The solution is expanded in a truncated PCE of total order \(d\),

$$ p(t,x,\gamma_x) \approx \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} a_j(t,x)\,\Phi_j(\gamma_x), \qquad L = \frac{(n_\theta + d)!}{n_\theta!\,d!} , $$

substituted into the PIDE, multiplied by each basis polynomial \(\Phi_k\), and averaged over the parameter distribution. Orthonormality (\(\langle\Phi_j\Phi_k\rangle = \delta_{jk}\)) collapses the time-derivative and burst terms onto mode \(k\), while the degradation term keeps full coupling through the moments \(\langle\gamma_x\Phi_j\Phi_k\rangle\). In vector form, with \(\mathbf{a}^L = (a_0,\dots,a_{L-1})^\top\):

$$ \frac{\partial \mathbf{a}^L}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big[x\,\mathbf{V}\,\mathbf{a}^L\big] + \boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{a}^L), \qquad V_{kj} := \langle \gamma_x\, \Phi_k \Phi_j \rangle . $$

\(\mathbf{V}\) depends only on the distribution of \(\gamma_x\) and the basis, so it is precomputed once. The key structural point: because \(\mathcal{B}\) does not depend on \(\gamma_x\), the burst term introduces no mode coupling in the one-parameter case.

Two uncertain parameters

With \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = [\gamma_x, k_m]\) independent, the basis is a tensor product \(\Phi_\alpha(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \phi_{\alpha_1}(\gamma_x)\phi_{\alpha_2}(k_m)\), and the burst operator factors as \(\mathcal{B}(p,k_m) = k_m(\mathcal{K}p)\). Now the burst term does couple modes:

$$ \frac{\partial \mathbf{a}^L}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big[x\,\mathbf{V}\,\mathbf{a}^L\big] + \mathbf{M}\,\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{a}^L), \qquad M_{k\alpha} := \langle k_m\,\Phi_k\Phi_\alpha \rangle . $$

That extra coupling is why the two-parameter case needs a higher expansion order to reach the same PDF accuracy.

Why intrusive PCE at all

Once the coefficients \(a_j(t,x)\) are available, the mean and variance come straight out of them, \(\mathbb{E}[p]\approx a_0\) and \(\mathrm{Var}[p]\approx\sum_{|i|\ge 1} a_i^2\langle\Phi_i^2\rangle\), with no further sampling. Because the coefficients are stored as time-resolved fields, the full PDF for any parameter realisation at any stored time is a polynomial evaluation. That is what makes the downstream control loop affordable: the controller reads \(\mathbb{E}[p]\) from the ensemble at every step.

Convergence and computational cost What refines the answer, what only costs time.

The short version: in the one-parameter problem the stochastic truncation error is already converged at the lowest order tested, so the accuracy is limited by the spatial grid. In the two-parameter problem that is no longer true.

Where the error actually lives

The domain is \(t\in[0,20]\), \(x\in[0,300]\), with a standard Gaussian initial condition. Grids of \(N\in\{75,\dots,1500\}\) points and PCE orders \(d\in\{2,\dots,6\}\) were swept, with Monte Carlo over \(10^4\) samples (generated with SELANSI) as the reference.

Refining the mesh from \(N=75\) to \(N=300\) removes most of the \(L^2\) spatial error; beyond \(N\approx 1200\) further refinement buys very little. Raising the PCE order from \(d=2\) to \(d=6\), by contrast, barely moves the mean and variance at all. Both observations point the same way: for a single uniform uncertain parameter, an order-2 expansion is already spectrally converged, and the dominant error is the spatial discretisation.

Consequence for cost. If the stochastic error is converged at \(d=2\), then paying for \(d=6\) is paying for nothing. At \(N=1501\) that is the difference between 911 s and 8107 s — a factor of about 9 for no gain in the quantities of interest.

Full-PDF accuracy, not just moments

Mean and variance are a weak test when the distribution can change shape. The PCE surrogate was therefore compared against the reference at \(t=20\) on the full PDF, ranked by total variation distance (mass redistribution) and Hellinger distance (shape, via square-root densities).

Where the worst case sits, and why

In the two-parameter problem the best and median cases are in the bimodal regime, where the surrogate captures both modes well. The worst case is in the strictly unimodal regime, furthest from the ensemble mean behaviour.

That ordering is the interesting part. For low \(\gamma_x\) the PDF can be bimodal for particular \(k_m\); as \(\gamma_x\) grows the second mode disappears, and the location of that transition depends on \(k_m\). Near the transition the map from parameters to solution is close to non-smooth, and a global polynomial basis converges slowly there — a well-documented limitation of global PCE. This is what forces \(d=3\) in the two-parameter case where \(d=2\) sufficed for one, and it is the argument for multi-element or adaptive stochastic Galerkin schemes with local refinement, or sparse PCE constructions, as the next step.

Against Monte Carlo

The most expensive configuration tested (\(N=1501\), \(d=6\)) finishes in 8107 s, about 2.25 h. Building the \(10^4\)-sample Monte Carlo reference with SELANSI needs roughly \(10^4\) deterministic runs at about 2 s each — on the order of 20 000 s, about 5.6 h.

The wall-clock ratio is real but it is not the main argument. The main argument is what happens after the solve: with the coefficients in hand, statistics and new parameter realisations cost a polynomial evaluation. Any task that needs many repeated evaluations — optimisation, parameter inference, sensitivity analysis, or the closed-loop controller on the control section — pays the solve once and then queries for free. Monte Carlo pays again every time.

Caveat on the timings. These are wall-clock times from one machine and one implementation, not a tuned benchmark. Treat the scaling trends as the result and the absolute seconds as indicative.
Control law and robustness Tracking from the ensemble mean, and PCE-aware vs nominal feedback.

Feedback from the ensemble mean

The controlled density is expanded in the orthonormal PCE basis \(\{\Phi_j(\gamma_x)\}_{j=0}^{L-1}\) of the uncertain degradation rate, \(p(t,x,\gamma_x)\approx\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} a_j(t,x)\,\Phi_j(\gamma_x)\). The feedback is driven by the ensemble-mean density, obtained from the zeroth PCE coefficient:

$$ \mu(x,t) \;:=\; a_0(t,x) \;=\; \Ex_{\gamma_x}\!\big[p(t,x,\gamma_x)\big], \qquad \int_{0}^{L_X}\mu(x,t)\dd x = 1 . $$

The controller needs \(\mathbb{E}[p]\) at every step; with an intrusive PCE it is already sitting in the state vector.

Tracking error, one dimension

Mean tracking

With a target mean protein level \(\mu^\star\),

$$ e_{\mathrm{mean}}(t) \;=\; \mu^\star-\int_{0}^{L_X} x\,\mu(x,t)\dd x . $$

Distribution tracking

Tracking a whole target shape \(p^\star(x)\) (normalised, \(\int p^\star\dd x = 1\)) is the harder problem, because a single scalar has to summarise a mismatch between two functions. The construction used here splits that into a direction, taken from the displacement of the means, and a magnitude, taken from a distribution distance:

$$ e_{\mathrm{pdf}}(t) \;=\; \sgn\!\left(\int_{0}^{L_X} x\,\big[p^\star(x)-\mu(x,t)\big]\dd x\right) \cdot D\big(p^\star,\mu(\cdot,t)\big), $$

with the distance \(D\) chosen from

$$ D_{L^2}=\!\left(\int_0^{L_X}\!(p^\star-\mu)^2\dd x\right)^{\!1/2}\!, \quad D_{\TV}=\tfrac12\!\int_0^{L_X}\!\big|p^\star-\mu\big|\dd x, \quad D_{\mathrm{MSE}}=\int_0^{L_X}\!(p^\star-\mu)^2\dd x . $$

This is the signal that chatters. Every distance above is non-negative and floors at the residual shape mismatch, which a single global inducer cannot drive to zero. Near steady state the magnitude therefore stops decreasing while the sign function keeps flipping as the mean crosses the target — so \(e_{\mathrm{pdf}}\) oscillates rather than settling. That is why the deployment below is run in lock-step rather than by replaying a saved \(I(t)\).

Inducer control law

With \(e(t)\in\{e_{\mathrm{mean}}(t),\,e_{\mathrm{pdf}}(t)\}\):

$$ u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)= \begin{cases} u_0 + K_p\,e(t), & \text{(P)}\\[4pt] u_0 + K_p\,e(t) + K_i\displaystyle\int_0^t e(\tau)\dd\tau . & \text{(PI)} \end{cases} $$

As implemented, the command is saturated to the admissible inducer range, \(u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)\leftarrow \sat_{[u_{\min},\,u_{\max}]}\big(u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)\big)\), with \(\sat_{[a,b]}(z)=\max\big(a,\min(b,z)\big)\).

The integral action is what makes the steady-state result insensitive to the exact gains — a point that matters for the robustness comparison below, since both controllers must share gains for the comparison to isolate the feedback source.

Two-dimensional toggle switch

The same construction extends to a two-gene mutual-repression network — the classic toggle switch. Two genes each express a protein; each protein represses the other gene, which is what makes the system bistable. Here the state is a joint density \(p(t,x_1,x_2)\) over both protein levels, and there are two inducers instead of one.

Mean joint density and its marginals

Let \(\mu(x_1,x_2,t):=a_0(t,x_1,x_2)=\Ex\big[p(t,x_1,x_2,\boldsymbol{\theta})\big]\) be the mean joint density — again the zeroth PCE coefficient. Its two marginals are

$$ \mu_1(x_1,t)=\int \mu(x_1,x_2,t)\dd x_2, \qquad \mu_2(x_2,t)=\int \mu(x_1,x_2,t)\dd x_1 . $$

Each marginal mean is normalised by its own marginal mass:

$$ \bar{x}_1(t)=\frac{\displaystyle\int x_1\,\mu_1(x_1,t)\dd x_1}{\displaystyle\int \mu_1(x_1,t)\dd x_1}, \qquad \bar{x}_2(t)=\frac{\displaystyle\int x_2\,\mu_2(x_2,t)\dd x_2}{\displaystyle\int \mu_2(x_2,t)\dd x_2}. $$

The normalisation is not cosmetic: it keeps the error robust to the small numerical mass loss that the truncated domain and the weak boundary treatment introduce.

Per-channel error and the decentralized law

With targets \(\mu_1^\star,\mu_2^\star\) and actuation sign \(s_k\in\{+1,-1\}\) (\(+1\) activating inducer, \(-1\) repressing):

$$ e_k(t)=s_k\big(\mu_k^\star-\bar{x}_k(t)\big),\qquad k=1,2 . $$

Two inducers \(I=[\,I_1,\,I_2\,]^\top\), one independent loop per protein — no cross-terms, no joint design:

$$ I_k(t)=\sat_{[u_{\min,k},\,u_{\max,k}]}\big(v_k(t)\big),\qquad v_k(t)= \begin{cases} u_{0,k}+K_{p,k}\,e_k(t), & \text{(P)}\\[4pt] u_{0,k}+K_{p,k}\,e_k(t)+K_{i,k}\displaystyle\int_0^t e_k(\tau)\dd\tau . & \text{(PI)} \end{cases} $$

Anti-windup

The integral term uses conditional-integration anti-windup: on channel \(k\) the integrator is frozen — not updated — whenever the unsaturated command exceeds a limit while the error still drives it further into saturation, that is when

$$ \big(v_k \gt u_{\max,k}\ \wedge\ e_k \gt 0\big) \quad\text{or}\quad \big(v_k \lt u_{\min,k}\ \wedge\ e_k \lt 0\big). $$

Without this, a channel pinned at a saturation limit keeps accumulating error it cannot act on, and the loop overshoots badly on the way back. It matters more here than in the one-dimensional case, because the toggle switch's bistability means one channel can sit hard against a limit for a long stretch while the other moves.

Reading the inducer arrows. In the schematic each inducer is drawn acting on the protein with the other index — \(I_2\) on \(P_1\), \(I_1\) on \(P_2\) — but the net effect of \(I_k\) is on channel \(k\). The inducer acts by sequestration, entering \(c_k\) as an apparent shift of the Hill threshold rather than as a removal term for the opposite protein: \(I_1\) appears only in \(c_1\), never in \(c_2\). Raising \(I_1\) raises \(K_{12}\), lowers the repression fraction and so raises \(x_1\). Hence \(\partial\bar{x}_k/\partial I_k \gt 0\) and \(s_k = +1\) for both channels, with \(\partial\bar{x}_k/\partial I_j \lt 0\) off-diagonal.

Does the ensemble mean help? The experiment

Is a PCE-aware controller more robust than a nominal-model-feedback controller for PDF-shape tracking, under \(\gamma_x \sim \mathcal{U}[\rho_{\min}, \rho_{\max}]\)?

Both controllers share the same structure and the same gains. They differ in one thing only — what they look at:

The only difference between the two controllers is the feedback source.
ControllerFeedback signalBlind to uncertainty?
PCE-awarePCE ensemble mean \(a_0(x) = \mathbb{E}[p]\)No
NominalDeterministic model at \(\gamma_x = c_1\) (midpoint)Yes

Each controller produces an inducer trajectory \(I(t)\), which is then deployed on the true uncertain ensemble. Per-realisation terminal PDFs \(p(x;\gamma_x)\) are reconstructed from the ensemble PCE coefficients over sampled \(\gamma_x\) and compared to the target \(p^*(x)\).

Deployment is live, not replayed. As noted above, \(e_{\mathrm{pdf}}\) chatters near steady state: the sign flips every step while the magnitude floors at the residual shape mismatch. Replaying a coarsely sampled chattering trajectory aliases it — matching required roughly 9000 samples in testing. Instead the nominal feedback model is integrated in lock-step with the ensemble and the same \(I\) is applied to both.

The margin grows with uncertainty

Nominal bias grows roughly quadratically, because the Jensen gap scales with \(\mathrm{Var}(\gamma_x)\), which scales with the square of the spread. PCE bias stays near zero across the whole range.

Two honest limitations

1. One scalar actuator cannot narrow the ensemble

The ensemble mean of a set of distributions is a mixture, and a mixture is always wider than any single member. A single global inducer shifts the whole family; it cannot compress it. So the PCE controller's advantage shows up in bias and worst case, not in the aggregate ensemble-mean PDF distance — which is dominated by that irreducible width floor and is therefore roughly tied at ±20%. This is a limitation of one scalar actuator, not of the method.

2. The autoregulation actuator (Mode A) is poorly conditioned

Mode A modulates the protein's own Hill self-regulation through \(K_{\text{eff}}(I)\) rather than acting as a pure inducer. Two problems appear.

Authority is regime-dependent. With the original parameters (\(K=45\), \(H=-4\)) the inducer barely moves the distribution: the mean is confined to roughly \([136,139]\), while \(\gamma_x\) alone swings it from 125 to 153. The Hill term is saturated (\(c\approx1\)) across the entire inducer range, so the control problem is nearly degenerate. Raising \(K\) with the inducer inverts the plant gain (\(I\uparrow \Rightarrow\) mean \(\downarrow\)), fighting the controller's sign convention. To recover authority with the correct sign the threshold has to sweep the protein range — \(K=150\), \(H=-4\) gives a reachable mean over roughly \([38,139]\).

Even with authority it is stiff. The mean is hypersensitive to \(\gamma_x\) at the operating point, so the deployed ensemble is bimodal and very wide, the closed loop does not fully settle (\(\mathbb{E}[x]\) drifts to about 102 rather than 100), and one global inducer cannot shape it.

Bottom line

The PCE-aware controller beats the nominal one in both modes — lower bias at every uncertainty level, with the gap widening as uncertainty grows. But Mode B (inducer-only) is where the framework is clean: there the PCE controller drives ensemble bias to nearly zero while the nominal bias grows quadratically. Mode A is a poorly-conditioned actuator with almost no authority at the original parameters and a large irreducible spread even when retuned, so the robustness margin is small. That is a property of the Mode-A actuator, not of the PCE method.

What these numbers depend on. Absolute values depend on the target \(p^*\), the gains, and the grid. The relative PCE-vs-nominal comparison is the result. Gains were tuned for convergent tracking; the PI integral action makes steady-state tracking insensitive to their exact values.
