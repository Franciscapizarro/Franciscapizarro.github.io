Two things on this page. First the control law itself: how a scalar tracking error is constructed from the PCE ensemble-mean density, and how it drives the inducer, in both the one-dimensional self-regulation case and the two-dimensional toggle switch. Then the experiment that tests whether feeding back the ensemble mean is actually more robust than feeding back a nominal model.

Feedback from the ensemble mean

The controlled density is expanded in the orthonormal PCE basis \(\{\Phi_j(\gamma_x)\}_{j=0}^{L-1}\) of the uncertain degradation rate, \(p(t,x,\gamma_x)\approx\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} a_j(t,x)\,\Phi_j(\gamma_x)\). The feedback is driven by the ensemble-mean density, which is exactly the zeroth PCE coefficient — no extra computation, no sampling:

$$ \mu(x,t) \;:=\; a_0(t,x) \;=\; \Ex_{\gamma_x}\!\big[p(t,x,\gamma_x)\big], \qquad \int_{0}^{L_X}\mu(x,t)\dd x = 1 . $$

That identity is the whole reason the loop is affordable. The controller needs \(\mathbb{E}[p]\) at every step; with an intrusive PCE it is already sitting in the state vector.

Tracking error, one dimension

Mean tracking

With a target mean protein level \(\mu^\star\),

$$ e_{\mathrm{mean}}(t) \;=\; \mu^\star-\int_{0}^{L_X} x\,\mu(x,t)\dd x . $$

Distribution tracking

Tracking a whole target shape \(p^\star(x)\) (normalised, \(\int p^\star\dd x = 1\)) is the harder problem, because a single scalar has to summarise a mismatch between two functions. The construction used here splits that into a direction, taken from the displacement of the means, and a magnitude, taken from a distribution distance:

$$ e_{\mathrm{pdf}}(t) \;=\; \sgn\!\left(\int_{0}^{L_X} x\,\big[p^\star(x)-\mu(x,t)\big]\dd x\right) \cdot D\big(p^\star,\mu(\cdot,t)\big), $$

with the distance \(D\) chosen from

$$ D_{L^2}=\!\left(\int_0^{L_X}\!(p^\star-\mu)^2\dd x\right)^{\!1/2}\!, \quad D_{\TV}=\tfrac12\!\int_0^{L_X}\!\big|p^\star-\mu\big|\dd x, \quad D_{\mathrm{MSE}}=\int_0^{L_X}\!(p^\star-\mu)^2\dd x . $$

This is the signal that chatters. Every distance above is non-negative and floors at the residual shape mismatch, which a single global inducer cannot drive to zero. Near steady state the magnitude therefore stops decreasing while the sign function keeps flipping as the mean crosses the target — so \(e_{\mathrm{pdf}}\) oscillates rather than settling. That is why the deployment below is run in lock-step rather than by replaying a saved \(I(t)\).

Inducer control law

With \(e(t)\in\{e_{\mathrm{mean}}(t),\,e_{\mathrm{pdf}}(t)\}\):

$$ u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)= \begin{cases} u_0 + K_p\,e(t), & \text{(P)}\\[4pt] u_0 + K_p\,e(t) + K_i\displaystyle\int_0^t e(\tau)\dd\tau . & \text{(PI)} \end{cases} $$

As implemented, the command is saturated to the admissible inducer range, \(u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)\leftarrow \sat_{[u_{\min},\,u_{\max}]}\big(u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)\big)\), with \(\sat_{[a,b]}(z)=\max\big(a,\min(b,z)\big)\).

The integral action is what makes the steady-state result insensitive to the exact gains — a point that matters for the robustness comparison below, since both controllers must share gains for the comparison to isolate the feedback source.

Two-dimensional toggle switch

The same construction extends to a two-gene mutual-repression network — the classic toggle switch. Two genes each express a protein; each protein represses the other gene, which is what makes the system bistable. Here the state is a joint density \(p(t,x_1,x_2)\) over both protein levels, and there are two inducers instead of one.

Mean joint density and its marginals

Let \(\mu(x_1,x_2,t):=a_0(t,x_1,x_2)=\Ex\big[p(t,x_1,x_2,\boldsymbol{\theta})\big]\) be the mean joint density — again the zeroth PCE coefficient. Its two marginals are

$$ \mu_1(x_1,t)=\int \mu(x_1,x_2,t)\dd x_2, \qquad \mu_2(x_2,t)=\int \mu(x_1,x_2,t)\dd x_1 . $$

Each marginal mean is normalised by its own marginal mass:

$$ \bar{x}_1(t)=\frac{\displaystyle\int x_1\,\mu_1(x_1,t)\dd x_1}{\displaystyle\int \mu_1(x_1,t)\dd x_1}, \qquad \bar{x}_2(t)=\frac{\displaystyle\int x_2\,\mu_2(x_2,t)\dd x_2}{\displaystyle\int \mu_2(x_2,t)\dd x_2}. $$

The normalisation is not cosmetic: it keeps the error robust to the small numerical mass loss that the truncated domain and the weak boundary treatment introduce.

Per-channel error and the decentralized law

With targets \(\mu_1^\star,\mu_2^\star\) and actuation sign \(s_k\in\{+1,-1\}\) (\(+1\) activating inducer, \(-1\) repressing):

$$ e_k(t)=s_k\big(\mu_k^\star-\bar{x}_k(t)\big),\qquad k=1,2 . $$

Two inducers \(I=[\,I_1,\,I_2\,]^\top\), one independent loop per protein — no cross-terms, no joint design:

$$ I_k(t)=\sat_{[u_{\min,k},\,u_{\max,k}]}\big(v_k(t)\big),\qquad v_k(t)= \begin{cases} u_{0,k}+K_{p,k}\,e_k(t), & \text{(P)}\\[4pt] u_{0,k}+K_{p,k}\,e_k(t)+K_{i,k}\displaystyle\int_0^t e_k(\tau)\dd\tau . & \text{(PI)} \end{cases} $$

Anti-windup

The integral term uses conditional-integration anti-windup: on channel \(k\) the integrator is frozen — not updated — whenever the unsaturated command exceeds a limit while the error still drives it further into saturation, that is when

$$ \big(v_k \gt u_{\max,k}\ \wedge\ e_k \gt 0\big) \quad\text{or}\quad \big(v_k \lt u_{\min,k}\ \wedge\ e_k \lt 0\big). $$

Without this, a channel pinned at a saturation limit keeps accumulating error it cannot act on, and the loop overshoots badly on the way back. It matters more here than in the one-dimensional case, because the toggle switch's bistability means one channel can sit hard against a limit for a long stretch while the other moves.

Check the inducer indexing on the schematic. The controller pairs each inducer with its own channel: \(I_1\) drives \(e_1 = s_1(\mu_1^\star-\bar{x}_1)\), and \(I_2\) drives \(e_2\). In the figure above, however, the inhibition arrows are drawn crossed — \(I_2\) lands on \(P_1\) and \(I_1\) on \(P_2\). Either the figure labels are swapped, or the intent is indirect actuation through the mutual repression, in which case the equations' pairing needs revisiting. Worth resolving before the figure goes into the paper; it is a labelling question, not a problem with the law itself.

Does the ensemble mean help? The experiment

Is a PCE-aware controller more robust than a nominal-model-feedback controller for PDF-shape tracking, under \(\gamma_x \sim \mathcal{U}[\rho_{\min}, \rho_{\max}]\)?

Both controllers share the same structure and the same gains. They differ in one thing only — what they look at:

The only difference between the two controllers is the feedback source.
ControllerFeedback signalBlind to uncertainty?
PCE-awarePCE ensemble mean \(a_0(x) = \mathbb{E}[p]\)No

Each controller produces an inducer trajectory \(I(t)\), which is then deployed on the true uncertain ensemble. Per-realisation terminal PDFs \(p(x;\gamma_x)\) are reconstructed from the ensemble PCE coefficients over sampled \(\gamma_x\) and compared to the target \(p^*(x)\).

Deployment is live, not replayed. As noted above, \(e_{\mathrm{pdf}}\) chatters near steady state: the sign flips every step while the magnitude floors at the residual shape mismatch. Replaying a coarsely sampled chattering trajectory aliases it — matching required roughly 9000 samples in testing. Instead the nominal feedback model is integrated in lock-step with the ensemble and the same \(I\) is applied to both.

The margin grows with uncertainty

Nominal bias grows roughly quadratically, because the Jensen gap scales with \(\mathrm{Var}(\gamma_x)\), which scales with the square of the spread. PCE bias stays near zero across the whole range.

Two honest limitations

1. One scalar actuator cannot narrow the ensemble

The ensemble mean of a set of distributions is a mixture, and a mixture is always wider than any single member. A single global inducer shifts the whole family; it cannot compress it. So the PCE controller's advantage shows up in bias and worst case, not in the aggregate ensemble-mean PDF distance — which is dominated by that irreducible width floor and is therefore roughly tied at ±20%. This is a limitation of one scalar actuator, not of the method.

2. The autoregulation actuator (Mode A) is poorly conditioned

Mode A modulates the protein's own Hill self-regulation through \(K_{\text{eff}}(I)\) rather than acting as a pure inducer. Two problems appear.

Authority is regime-dependent. With the original parameters (\(K=45\), \(H=-4\)) the inducer barely moves the distribution: the mean is confined to roughly \([136,139]\), while \(\gamma_x\) alone swings it from 125 to 153. The Hill term is saturated (\(c\approx1\)) across the entire inducer range, so the control problem is nearly degenerate. Raising \(K\) with the inducer inverts the plant gain (\(I\uparrow \Rightarrow\) mean \(\downarrow\)), fighting the controller's sign convention. To recover authority with the correct sign the threshold has to sweep the protein range — \(K=150\), \(H=-4\) gives a reachable mean over roughly \([38,139]\).

Even with authority it is stiff. The mean is hypersensitive to \(\gamma_x\) at the operating point, so the deployed ensemble is bimodal and very wide, the closed loop does not fully settle (\(\mathbb{E}[x]\) drifts to about 102 rather than 100), and one global inducer cannot shape it.

Bottom line

The PCE-aware controller beats the nominal one in both modes — lower bias at every uncertainty level, with the gap widening as uncertainty grows. But Mode B (inducer-only) is where the framework is clean: there the PCE controller drives ensemble bias to nearly zero while the nominal bias grows quadratically. Mode A is a poorly-conditioned actuator with almost no authority at the original parameters and a large irreducible spread even when retuned, so the robustness margin is small. That is a property of the Mode-A actuator, not of the PCE method.

What these numbers depend on. Absolute values depend on the target \(p^*\), the gains, and the grid. The relative PCE-vs-nominal comparison is the result. Gains were tuned for convergent tracking; the PI integral action makes steady-state tracking insensitive to their exact values.
