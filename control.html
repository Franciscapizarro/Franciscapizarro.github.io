The poster claims that closing the loop on the PCE ensemble mean removes nominal-model bias and lowers worst-case error. This page gives the experiment, the numbers behind that claim, and the two places where the advantage is small or absent.

The question and the design

Is a PCE-aware controller more robust than a nominal-model-feedback controller for PDF-shape tracking, under \(\gamma_x \sim \mathcal{U}[\rho_{\min}, \rho_{\max}]\)?

Both controllers share the same structure and the same gains. They differ in one thing only — what they look at:

The only difference between the two controllers is the feedback source.
ControllerFeedback signalBlind to uncertainty?
PCE-awarePCE ensemble mean \(a_0(x) = \mathbb{E}[p]\)No
NominalDeterministic model at \(\gamma_x = c_1\) (midpoint)Yes

Each controller produces an inducer trajectory \(I(t)\), which is then deployed on the true uncertain ensemble. Per-realisation terminal PDFs \(p(x;\gamma_x)\) are reconstructed from the ensemble PCE coefficients over sampled \(\gamma_x\) and compared to the target \(p^*(x)\).

Deployment is live, not replayed. The error signal \(\mathrm{sign}(\text{mean gap})\cdot\|p^*-p\|\) chatters near steady state: the sign flips every step while the magnitude floors at the residual shape mismatch. Replaying a coarsely sampled chattering trajectory aliases it — matching required roughly 9000 samples in testing. Instead the nominal feedback model is integrated in lock-step with the ensemble and the same \(I\) is applied to both.

The margin grows with uncertainty

Nominal bias grows roughly quadratically, because the Jensen gap scales with \(\mathrm{Var}(\gamma_x)\), which scales with the square of the spread. PCE bias stays near zero across the whole range.

Two honest limitations

1. One scalar actuator cannot narrow the ensemble

The ensemble mean of a set of distributions is a mixture, and a mixture is always wider than any single member. A single global inducer shifts the whole family; it cannot compress it. So the PCE controller's advantage shows up in bias and worst case, not in the aggregate ensemble-mean PDF distance — which is dominated by that irreducible width floor and is therefore roughly tied at ±20%. This is a limitation of one scalar actuator, not of the method.

2. The autoregulation actuator (Mode A) is poorly conditioned

Mode A modulates the protein's own Hill self-regulation through \(K_{\text{eff}}(I)\) rather than acting as a pure inducer. Two problems appear.

Authority is regime-dependent. With the original parameters (\(K=45\), \(H=-4\)) the inducer barely moves the distribution: the mean is confined to roughly \([136,139]\), while \(\gamma_x\) alone swings it from 125 to 153. The Hill term is saturated (\(c\approx1\)) across the entire inducer range, so the control problem is nearly degenerate. Raising \(K\) with the inducer inverts the plant gain (\(I\uparrow \Rightarrow\) mean \(\downarrow\)), fighting the controller's sign convention. To recover authority with the correct sign the threshold has to sweep the protein range — \(K=150\), \(H=-4\) gives a reachable mean over roughly \([38,139]\).

Even with authority it is stiff. The mean is hypersensitive to \(\gamma_x\) at the operating point, so the deployed ensemble is bimodal and very wide, the closed loop does not fully settle (\(\mathbb{E}[x]\) drifts to about 102 rather than 100), and one global inducer cannot shape it.

Bottom line

The PCE-aware controller beats the nominal one in both modes — lower bias at every uncertainty level, with the gap widening as uncertainty grows. But Mode B (inducer-only) is where the framework is clean: there the PCE controller drives ensemble bias to nearly zero while the nominal bias grows quadratically. Mode A is a poorly-conditioned actuator with almost no authority at the original parameters and a large irreducible spread even when retuned, so the robustness margin is small. That is a property of the Mode-A actuator, not of the PCE method.

What these numbers depend on. Absolute values depend on the target \(p^*\), the gains, and the grid. The relative PCE-vs-nominal comparison is the result. Gains were tuned for convergent tracking; the PI integral action makes steady-state tracking insensitive to their exact values. See Code & reproduction for the verification that was performed.
